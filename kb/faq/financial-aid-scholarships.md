# Does applying for financial aid hurt my chances of getting accepted?

For 'need-blind' universities (like Harvard, Princeton, Yale, Stanford, MIT, and many top liberal arts colleges), a family's financial need is not considered during admissions evaluation. For 'need-aware' schools, financial need may be a factor, particularly for borderline candidates. We help families navigate need-blind vs need-aware dynamics.

# Can middle and upper-middle income families receive financial aid or scholarships?

Yes! Many private universities have extensive non-need-based merit scholarships awarded purely on academic and extracurricular talent, regardless of income. Additionally, universities with massive endowments often provide need-based grants to families with incomes up to $150k-$250k. We help identify colleges where your student is in the top 10% of applicants, maximizing merit award potential.

# Can you appeal a financial aid package if the initial offer is insufficient?

Yes. Financial aid offers can be formally appealed, especially if there has been a change in financial circumstances, unexpected medical expenses, or if you have competing merit offers from peer institutions. We provide families with proven appeal letter frameworks.
