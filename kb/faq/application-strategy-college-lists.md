# How many colleges should a high school senior typically apply to?

We typically recommend a balanced list of 10 to 14 colleges: 2 to 3 'Likely' schools (where admit rate is high and stats are well above the 75th percentile), 4 to 6 'Target' schools (where credentials align with middle 50%), and 3 to 5 'Reach' or highly selective schools (including Ivies, UCs, and top national universities). Applying to too many schools dilutes essay quality and leads to application fatigue.

# What is the difference between Early Decision (ED), Early Action (EA), and Regular Decision (RD)?

Early Decision (ED) is legally binding-if accepted, you must enroll and withdraw all other applications. It typically provides a meaningful statistical advantage at schools that value yield. Early Action (EA) is non-binding, allowing you to apply early and receive an early decision without commitment. Regular Decision (RD) has standard January deadlines with decisions in late March or April.

# When should high school students start the college planning process?

The earlier, the better. While junior and senior years focus on essays and applications, 9th and 10th grades are when crucial foundations are built-course rigor, leadership development, passion projects, and summer research. Starting early eliminates last-minute stress and ensures a coherent academic spike.
