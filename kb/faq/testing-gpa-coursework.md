# Are colleges truly test-optional, and should my student submit their score?

While many schools remain test-optional, an increasing number of elite institutions (MIT, Dartmouth, Yale, UT Austin, Georgetown) have reinstated test requirements. At test-optional schools, submitting a score at or above the school's median 50% percentile strongly bolsters an application. We evaluate your score against each college's specific profile to advise whether to submit or withhold.

# Is it better to get a B in an AP class or an A in a regular class?

As selective admissions deans frequently state: 'It is best to get an A in an AP class!' Highly selective colleges look for students who maximize the academic rigor available at their high school. A 'B' in an AP course is generally preferred over an unchallenging course load, but course selection should be balanced to maintain student well-being.

# How does the Digital SAT differ from the old paper test?

The Digital SAT is adaptive: your performance on the first module determines the difficulty level of questions in the second module. It is also significantly shorter (approx. 2 hours and 14 minutes) with shorter reading passages and a built-in Desmos graphing calculator for all math sections.
