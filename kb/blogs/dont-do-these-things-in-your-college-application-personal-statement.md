Here are some essential D os and Don'ts to keep in mind as you embark on your essay-writing journey.

# ON PRESENTATION

DON'T

DO!

Brag. Since your college application essays are less like a job interview and more like a meaningful "meet-and-greet," don't sprinkle your personal statement with awards or leadership titles. Your admissions reader will see your accolades in other parts of your application.
Show humility, vulnerability, and introspection. Share stories and reflections that demonstrate thoughtfulness, insight, and personal growth. Aim to make the admissions officer feel like they are getting to know a real, relatable person.

Feel compelled to start with a dramatic anecdote. Successful admissions essays provide engaging, authentic informational narratives. There is no need for literary flourishes that feel out of your writing comfort zone.
Be interesting, informative, and yourself. Ensure your essay is engaging and clearly answers the prompt. It's okay if your essay is straightforward, as long as it's well-written and interesting.

Dwell solely on the "Four D's" (death, divorce, drug/alcohol use, depression). Avoid writing an entire essay about how terrible an experience was.
Focus on growth and learning. When writing about difficult experiences, highlight what you learned and how you grew from the experience.

Write vague, unspecific sentences. Avoid generic statements like, "And then we did a lot of work."
Use concrete details to bring your experiences to life. "I delegated outreach tasks to Fred, Pete, and Joe, placed ads in our school paper, The Falcon, and gave morning announcements about our new hackathon."

Repeat your activities list. Use your Personal Statement to reveal something new about yourself that isn't evident from the rest of your application. Avoid writing about activities already highlighted elsewhere.
Reveal a new dimension of yourself. Use the Personal Statement to showcase aspects of your personality, interests, or experiences that aren't covered elsewhere in your application.

Use clichés. Avoid overused phrases and expressions like "the light at the end of the tunnel" or "a blessing in disguise."
Tell your story uniquely. Share your experiences in a way that only you can. Include personal insights and thoughtful details in language that feels comfortable to you. A story does not have to be fancy or dramatic to be really, really good.

Rely on a thesaurus to sound sophisticated. Don't use words that you wouldn't normally use.
Write in your own voice. Use language that feels natural to you. As long as your essay is grammatically correct and presents a logical narrative of growth and observation, it will be an asset to your application.

# ON PROCESS

DON'T

DO!

Skip reading the prompt (even if you think you are already familiar with it) before brainstorming.
Read each prompt carefully, marking it up, to make sure you are answering the question(s) asked.

Decide on a topic for one essay without considering the full array of essays for each application. Don't pick a topic for your Common Application Personal Statement without first thinking about the themes of the school's supplemental essays to avoid repeating material.
Plan your topics strategically. Consider the themes of all your essays before deciding on a topic for your Common App Personal Statement. Ensure each essay highlights different aspects of your personality, skills, and accomplishments.

Get too much help from others. It's not a good idea to rely too heavily on friends and family for ideas. Everyone will have different opinions about what you should write. This is your essay, and it needs to sound like you, not someone else's version of you.
Stay true to your voice. Seek feedback, but ensure the final essay reflects your authentic self. Your admissions reader is evaluating you. To achieve authenticity in your personal statement, it's best to get guidance from someone who does not know you well, as counterintuitive as that may seem. Like your admissions reader, this person will see you in a fresh way, as opposed to those who have a history with you or a rigid view of you.

Worry about word count in initial drafts. Don't focus on the word count when drafting your essays. Include all the information you want first, and then edit to fit the word limit.
Focus on content first. Write freely in your initial drafts without worrying about the word count. Once you have all your ideas down, you can edit and refine the content to meet the required length.

Mechanically copy and paste supplemental essays. Don't blindly reuse essays across different applications, even if the prompts are similar.
Customize each essay. If reusing some material from another essay, tailor it to reflect why you're a good fit for that particular institution. Proofread to make sure you're using the right name of the school.

Force yourself to start at the beginning. Don't get stuck trying to write the perfect opening paragraph first.
Start where you're comfortable. Begin with sections you find easier to write. This can help you build momentum and reduce the intimidation of starting with a perfect opening. Putting everything together at the end is the fun part!

Stress over whether your topic is "good enough." Don't worry about finding the perfect topic for your Common Application Personal Statement. Any topic can work if you write about it thoughtfully, showing its unique impact on you and what you learned.
Choose meaningful topics. Write about topics that are significant to you. What matters most is how you explore the topic, its impact on you, and what you learned from the experience.

Overanalyze "successful" essay examples. Don't obsess over examples of successful essays. What worked for someone else might not work for you.
Draw inspiration carefully. Look at examples of successful essays for inspiration, but don't try to replicate them. Focus on telling your own unique story in a way that is true to you.
