# Essential Legal Documents

- Durable Power of Attorney: A durable power of attorney allows you to handle financial and legal matters on behalf of your child. This document is crucial for situations in which your child may be unable to manage their financial affairs, such as handling banking transactions or signing legal documents.

- Advanced Healthcare Directive: Unlike a healthcare proxy, which primarily designates a representative to make medical decisions in unforeseen circumstances, an Advanced Health Care Directive delves into the intricacies of an individual's medical preferences and end-of-life decisions. This comprehensive document allows your college-bound student to articulate specific instructions regarding the type of medical treatments they wish to receive or avoid, ensuring their preferences are known and honored.

- Will and Testament: While it may seem premature, having a basic will in place is a responsible step for any legal adult. This becomes especially important if your child has significant assets, even if they are not financial in nature.

- FERPA Release Form: The Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA) protects the privacy of student education records. While your child is considered a legal adult in college, a FERPA release form allows them to grant you access to their academic records and communicate with college staff on their behalf.

- HIPAA Authorization: Before your child turns 18, you likely have the legal authority to make medical decisions on their behalf. However, once they enter college, they are considered legal adults, and their medical information becomes private. A healthcare proxy and HIPAA authorization form will grant you the ability to access their medical records and make healthcare decisions in case of emergencies.

# Approaching the Conversation with a Resistant Child

Initiating a conversation about legal documents with a resistant child can be challenging, as it may seem like an intrusion into their newfound independence. It's crucial to approach the topic with sensitivity and respect for their autonomy.

Begin by expressing your genuine concern for their well-being and explaining that these documents are designed to ensure their safety and support, especially in unforeseen circumstances. Emphasize that these measures aren't meant to restrict their freedom but rather to provide a safety net that respects their wishes.

Encourage an open dialogue, assuring them that you value their opinions and want to work together to make informed decisions.!
