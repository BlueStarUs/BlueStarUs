# Introduction

Assuming that all elite colleges value the same things in an applicant is an easy mistake to make. Strong GPA? Leadership? Research? While these elements matter, each top school has its own unique preferences -and understanding those preferences can make or break an application.

That's where strategic admissions planning comes in. To craft a winning application, you need insider knowledge about what each institution truly prioritizes. Enter the Common Data Set (CDS) -one of the most powerful yet overlooked tools in college admissions.

# Cracking the Code: What Elite Schools Really Want

Colleges don't randomly admit students-they follow patterns. The CDS is a standardized report that many schools publish, revealing exactly which factors carry the most weight in their admissions process .

- At some schools, GPA and course rigor rule. They care far more about academic strength than extracurriculars.

- Others emphasize a holistic approach. Essays, recommendations, and leadership experience carry as much weight as grades.

- Some prioritize demonstrated interest. If you haven't engaged with their admissions office, your chances drop.

By analyzing the CDS for your target schools, you can tailor your application strategy -ensuring you highlight what matters most to them .

# Why "One-Size-Fits-All" Advice Fails

Not all top schools value the same qualities. Here's why a generic approach won't help your child:

- MIT vs. Williams: MIT prioritizes math and science excellence; Williams values intellectual curiosity and writing skills.

- UCLA vs. Brown: UCLA heavily weighs GPA due to the UC system's structure; Brown looks for intellectual risk-takers.

- Public vs. Private Universities: Public schools may favor in-state students; private institutions might give weight to legacy applicants and demonstrated interest.

Trying to apply with a broad, generic strategy? That's a recipe for missed opportunities.
