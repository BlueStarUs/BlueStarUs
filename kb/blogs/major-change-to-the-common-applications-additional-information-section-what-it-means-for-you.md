# Introduction

Each year, the Common App undergoes slight modifications, but this year, one change in particular could significantly impact how students present themselves to admissions committees.

# The Big Change: Reduced Word Limit from 650 to 300 Words

Previously, students could use up to 650 words in the Additional Information section to provide context about extenuating circumstances, unique experiences, or anything that didn't fit elsewhere in their application. However, for the 2024-2025 application cycle, the word limit has been cut by more than half -students now have only 300 words to work with.

This is a big shift. The Additional Information section has long been a place where students could elaborate on aspects of their background that might not be fully captured in their essays, activities list, or letters of recommendation.

With this new constraint, applicants must be more strategic than ever in deciding what to include.

# How to Adapt: Strategic Approaches for Students

Given this reduced space, students should consider alternative ways to showcase key accomplishments and unique contexts that might have gone in this section previously. Here are some suggestions:

1. Use a Resume Where Allowed

Some colleges allow students to submit a resume along with their application. Schools like Georgetown and Cornell explicitly encourage this, and it can be an excellent way to provide a detailed overview of extracurriculars, work experience, and leadership roles.

- Check each school's Common Application Supplement section to confirm if a resume is permitted.

- If permitted, craft a well-structured resume that highlights key experiences without repeating details from the Education section, such as standardized test scores, GPA, course and AP scores, or academic honors, which are already included elsewhere in the Common Application.

2. Link to a Personal Website or Online Portfolio

A growing number of schools allow students to submit a website link to showcase projects, research, or creative work. This is different from official Art, Music, Writing, or Research supplemental portfolios, which have designated submission processes within many applications. While not all schools provide a specific field for a personal website, students can often include the link in the Additional Information section as a workaround.

- If you have a portfolio, blog, or personal website that highlights key experiences, consider linking to it.

- Since admissions officers are not obligated to click external links and may not do so, it will be advantageous to provide brief, compelling context that encourages them to explore further. A well-organized, professional online presence can add value if effectively introduced!

3. Prioritize What Matters Most in the Additional Information Section

With only 300 words available, students must be highly selective about what they include. Consider these priorities:

- Critical academic or personal circumstances: Use this space to briefly explain significant disruptions, like illness, family hardships, or challenges affecting academic performance.

- Clarifications not covered elsewhere: If your transcript or activities list lacks key context (e.g., a major shift in coursework or a non-traditional extracurricular), use this section to provide concise explanations.

- Highly relevant accomplishments: If a major achievement doesn't fit into the activities section, it may be worth including a brief mention here- but only if it adds new value.

# Final Takeaway

While this change limits how much additional information students can provide, there are still creative ways to highlight important details. Whether through a resume (when allowed), a website link, or strategic prioritization of information, applicants can still ensure admissions officers get a well-rounded view of their story.
