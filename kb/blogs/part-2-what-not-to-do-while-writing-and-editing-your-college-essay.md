# Introduction

You've selected your topic, thought through your strategy, and now you're ready to write. But even experienced writers can stumble here.

The most common mistake? Trying too hard to impress. Remember: colleges want to hear from a real person, not a thesaurus or résumé. The best essays are thoughtful, honest, and clearly told.

Below are the most common presentation mistakes students make while writing-and how to avoid them.

# ON PRESENTATION - Voice, Style & Structure

❌ DON'T

✅ DO

Brag. Don't sprinkle your essay with awards or leadership titles. Your achievements are already listed elsewhere.
Show humility, vulnerability, and introspection. Share stories that demonstrate growth and insight. Make the reader feel like they're meeting a real person.

Feel compelled to start with a dramatic anecdote.
Be interesting, informative, and yourself. Your essay doesn't need to be flashy-it just needs to be genuine and engaging.

Dwell solely on the "Four D's" (death, divorce, drug/alcohol use, depression).
Focus on growth and learning. If writing about a difficult experience, highlight what you learned and how you grew.

Write vague, unspecific sentences.
Use concrete details to bring your experiences to life. Show us what you did, who was involved, and what the outcome was.

Repeat your activities list.
Reveal a new dimension of yourself. Use this space to share something not seen elsewhere in your application.

Use clichés. Avoid phrases like "the light at the end of the tunnel."
Tell your story uniquely. Use thoughtful, personal language that reflects your voice and experience.

Rely on a thesaurus to sound sophisticated.
Write in your own voice. As long as your language is clear, natural, and grammatically correct, your message will shine.
