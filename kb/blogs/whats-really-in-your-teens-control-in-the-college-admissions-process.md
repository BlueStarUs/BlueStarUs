# Introduction

If you are the parent of a high-achieving, college-bound teen, you know how confusing the college admissions process can feel. With so many factors at play, it is easy to wonder which ones your child can influence and which ones are entirely out of their hands.

The reality is that while some aspects of admissions are beyond control, many of the most important areas can be shaped by a student's preparation, focus, and strategy. Understanding this distinction helps families use their energy where it matters most.

# The Factors That Matter Most: What's Really in a Student's Control

Contrary to the myths and chatter surrounding admissions, your teen has significant power over the story their application tells. Admissions officers consistently emphasize five areas where students can make the greatest impact:

- Academic GPA - Consistency and growth across years, paired with rigor in course selection, remain the cornerstone of admissions. Students may not be able to control how tough a grading system is, but they can control their effort, study habits, and resilience when courses become challenging.

- Standardized Tests - Even with test-optional policies, strong scores can boost an application. Students control how intentionally they prepare, whether through self-study, tutoring, or structured practice.

- Extracurricular Activities - Students can't do everything, but they can go deep. Committing to a few activities, taking on leadership, and pursuing genuine interests demonstrates character far more than a padded résumé.

- Recommendations - Students can influence recommendations by cultivating respectful, engaged relationships with teachers and counselors. When students show initiative and integrity in class, they make it easier for recommenders to write glowing letters.

- Application Essay - Essays are the clearest opportunity for students to speak directly to admissions officers. They can control the time they invest in reflection, drafting, revising, and finding their authentic voice.

In other words, students can't control outcomes -but they absolutely can control inputs : the choices, habits, and priorities that shape those outcomes.

# The Hybrid Factors: Partially Within Reach

Some components are partly in a student's hands. Athletic or artistic talent, for example, reflects years of practice, but opportunities to showcase them depend on competition and institutional needs. Interviews and demonstrated interest (through visits or early applications) give students a chance to shine, but outcomes hinge on how admissions committees weigh them.

# Beyond a Student's Control

Finally, some factors are completely outside a student's influence: legacy status, race or ethnicity, geography, or whether they're a first-generation applicant. While these can tip the scales, they should never distract families from focusing on where effort truly pays off.

A Clear Path for Families

Instead of losing energy over what cannot be changed, parents can support their teen by doubling down on what can: academic performance, meaningful activities, authentic essays, and thoughtful recommendations.
